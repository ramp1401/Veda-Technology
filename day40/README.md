# Customer Segmentation & Service Recommendation System

**Veda Technology — Data Science Internship (Day 1 of 4)**

## Overview
A beginner-friendly, reproducible data science project that uses synthetic customer behavior data, K-Means clustering, PCA visualization, segment profiles, and transparent rule-based service/learning recommendations.

## Business problem
Generic outreach may not fit customers with different engagement levels, purchase histories, support needs, and interests. This project explores how behavior-based groups can support more relevant education and service journeys.

## Objectives
- Generate at least 1,000 synthetic customer records.
- Clean data and explore behavior with charts.
- Engineer behavioral features and standardize numeric variables.
- Compare cluster counts using inertia and silhouette score.
- Profile clusters and generate explainable recommendations.
- Export CSV outputs and explore results in a Streamlit dashboard.

## Features and deliverables
- Synthetic customer dataset and data quality checks
- Exploratory analysis charts
- Feature engineering and StandardScaler preprocessing
- K-Means model selection using elbow and silhouette diagnostics
- PCA 2D cluster visualization
- Segment profiles and customer-level recommendations
- Streamlit dashboard with filters and CSV download
- Exported CSV outputs

## Technologies
Python, Pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Jupyter Notebook, Streamlit.

## Dataset and privacy
The notebook creates synthetic data with 1,200 initial customer records, including age, customer type, preferred program, visits, session duration, engagement, service inquiries, purchases, spending, support interactions, and satisfaction. It introduces a small amount of missingness and duplicate rows so cleaning can be demonstrated. The generator uses a fixed random seed.

**Synthetic-data disclaimer:** These are simulated records, not Veda Technology customer data. Any observed patterns or metrics are examples from synthetic data and must not be presented as real company findings.

## Methodology
1. **Cleaning:** remove duplicate rows and fill numeric missing values with medians; categorical missing values use the mode.
2. **Feature engineering:** calculate visits per purchase, inquiry rate per visit, and engagement per visit.
3. **StandardScaler:** scale numeric features so variables with larger units do not dominate Euclidean distances.
4. **K-Means:** assigns each customer to one of `k` clusters by iteratively updating cluster centers.
5. **Elbow method:** plots inertia for candidate `k` values. Look for diminishing improvements rather than treating the elbow as an exact rule.
6. **Silhouette score:** summarizes how close each point is to its own cluster compared with neighboring clusters. Higher is generally better, but it does not establish business value.
7. **PCA:** projects standardized features to two components for visualization. It is not a perfect representation of the full feature space.

The notebook chooses the candidate `k` with the highest silhouette score among k=2 through k=8, then prints the actual calculated score. Review the elbow plot and cluster profiles as well; the highest silhouette score is a baseline decision, not a universal truth.

## Customer segments
The notebook assigns segment descriptions from calculated cluster profiles using transparent heuristics:
- **Highly Engaged Customers:** above-median engagement and website visits.
- **Service-Focused Customers:** above-median service inquiries and support interactions, when the previous rules do not apply.
- **New or Low-Engagement Customers:** below-median visits and engagement, when earlier rules do not apply.
- **Occasional Visitors:** the fallback category.

These names are heuristic and may not map one-to-one to unique clusters; review profiles before business use. Cluster IDs themselves are arbitrary.

## Recommendation logic
Recommendations are rule-based and include the customer's preferred program:
- Highly engaged: advanced project mentoring or an advanced learning pathway.
- Service-focused: personalized consultation and guided onboarding.
- New/low engagement: beginner orientation and starter resources.
- Occasional: targeted webinar and curated intermediate pathway.
- Low satisfaction adds a follow-up to understand concerns before promotional outreach.

These service descriptions are illustrative suggestions, not a claim about the company's official catalogue. Validate them with stakeholders before publishing.

## Business recommendations
- Pilot the recommendations with opt-in customers and track enrollment, engagement, satisfaction, and opt-out rates.
- Offer helpful support before promotional outreach to customers with low satisfaction.
- Review cluster stability and profiles periodically.
- Avoid using age or other sensitive/proxy variables for consequential decisions without a careful fairness review.
- Validate all findings with authorized, representative data before operational use.

## Limitations and future improvements
- Synthetic data cannot establish real customer behavior.
- K-Means is distance-based, sensitive to outliers/scaling/`k`, and works best for relatively compact clusters.
- Silhouette score is only one diagnostic.
- Segment labels are heuristic and are not ground truth.
- Recommendation rules have not been tested against real outcomes.
- Future work: stability analysis, robust scaling/outlier handling, careful categorical encoding, business validation, recommendation A/B tests, privacy controls, model monitoring, and automated refreshes.

## Interview questions
1. **Why scale features?** K-Means relies on distances, so scaling prevents large-unit features from dominating.
2. **Why not include customer ID?** It is an identifier without behavioral meaning.
3. **What is inertia?** Sum of squared distances from samples to their cluster centers.
4. **What does silhouette score tell you?** How well a sample fits its own cluster compared with neighboring clusters.
5. **What is PCA?** A method that projects features into directions of high variance.
6. **Why use K-Means?** It is a simple baseline for numeric customer behavior vectors.
7. **What is a limitation of K-Means?** It is sensitive to scaling, outliers, and cluster count.
8. **Why impute missing values?** Most clustering estimators require complete numeric inputs.
9. **How to evaluate recommendations?** Measure outcomes in a controlled pilot, including satisfaction and opt-outs.
10. **What is data leakage?** Using information unavailable at prediction time or derived from the target.
11. **Why use synthetic data?** It demonstrates the workflow without exposing private records.
12. **How can this project improve?** Validate on authorized data, assess cluster stability, and monitor outcomes.
